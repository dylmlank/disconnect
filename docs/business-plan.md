## 1. Executive Summary

Disconnect is a mobile app that uses technology as a bridge to offline living. It connects like-minded people who want to spend less time on screens and more time in the real world — through local events, spontaneous meetups (beacons), and ephemeral group chats that disappear after events end.

Unlike every other social app, Disconnect is designed to be **opened briefly and closed quickly**. The best user session is under 2 minutes. There are no feeds, no infinite scroll, no engagement loops, and no algorithm-driven recommendations.

**Target market:** Gen Z (18-25) and Gen Alpha (13-17) in urban areas who are experiencing social media fatigue and want IRL connection.

**Business model:** Freemium B2C with a B2B expansion path.

**Ask:** Launch in one city, reach 1,000 active users in 3 months, validate monetization hypotheses, then raise a pre-seed round.

---

## 2. Problem

Social media platforms are engineered to maximize screen time. The result:

- **Gen Z spends 4.5+ hours/day** on social media (2025 Gallup data)
- **73% of Gen Z** report feeling "addicted" to their phones (Common Sense Media, 2024)
- **58% of young adults** say they want to spend less time on screens but don't know how (Deloitte Digital Media Trends, 2025)
- Social isolation is at epidemic levels — the U.S. Surgeon General declared it a public health crisis in 2023

**The paradox:** People who want to disconnect from screens have no technology-based tool to help them connect in the real world. Existing apps either:
- Focus on *limiting* screen time (Opal, One Sec, Screen Time) — they don't solve loneliness
- Claim to promote "real connection" but still rely on engagement loops (BeReal, Locket)
- Are event platforms without the anti-screen-time philosophy (Meetup, Eventbrite, Partiful)

**Gap in the market:** No app exists that is intentionally designed to get people OFF the app and into the world, with anti-engagement design as a core product principle.

---

## 3. Solution

Disconnect solves this with two core features:

### Events & Meetups
Users discover and join real-world events within their city (5-25 mile radius). Events have categories (outdoors, coffee, sports, games, study, creative, food, music, volunteering), max attendee caps, and ephemeral group chats that auto-delete 24 hours after the event.

### Beacons (Spontaneous Hangouts)
Users post short-lived invites ("I'm at the park, come join") that appear on a map and expire after 2 hours. Nearby users can tap to express interest — one tap, no messaging required.

### Anti-Screen-Time Design Principles
- No infinite scroll — all lists are finite and short
- No algorithmic recommendations — events are sorted by time and proximity
- 5-minute nudge — "You've been here a while. Pick something and go."
- Weekly screen time badge — gamifies spending LESS time in the app
- Ephemeral chats — auto-delete to prevent the app from becoming a messaging platform
- Limited notifications — event reminders only, no engagement bait

## 4. Market Analysis

### Target Market

**Primary:** Urban Gen Z (18-25) who are socially active but dissatisfied with social media.
**Secondary:** Gen Alpha (13-17) with age-appropriate restrictions.
**Tertiary (B2B):** Universities, coworking spaces, and city recreation departments looking for community engagement tools.

### Market Size

| Segment | Size | Source |
|---------|------|--------|
| U.S. Gen Z population (13-29) | ~70 million | Census Bureau |
| Digital wellness app market (global) | $3.5B by 2028 | Grand View Research |
| Social discovery / local events market | $1.2B (U.S.) | IBISWorld |
| Gen Z social app spend (annual, U.S.) | $5.8B | Sensor Tower |

**Serviceable addressable market (SAM):** Urban Gen Z in the U.S. who have expressed interest in reducing screen time — estimated at 12-15 million people.

**Serviceable obtainable market (SOM):** 50,000 users in Year 1 across 5 cities.

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | What They Do | Monetization | Gap Disconnect Fills |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|---------------------|
| **Meetup** | Event discovery + groups | Subscriptions ($14/mo for organizers) | Stale, aging user base (skews 35+), not mobile-first, no anti-screen-time ethos |
| **Eventbrite** | Event ticketing | Ticketing fees | Transactional, not community-focused, no spontaneous hangouts |
| **Partiful** | Event invitations | Free (VC-funded) | Requires knowing people already — not for meeting strangers |
| **BeReal** | Daily photo sharing | Ads (BeReal+) | Still a screen-time app, just with a different hook |
| **IRL** (shut down) | Social calendar | Failed | Faked user numbers, proved market interest but bad execution |
| **Timeleft** | Dinner meetups with strangers | Paid events (~$25/dinner) | High price point, only one format (dinner), no spontaneous option |
| **Opal / One Sec** | Screen time limiting | Subscriptions ($10/mo) | Only reduce screen time, don't replace it with anything |

**Key insight:** The screen-time-limiting apps (Opal, One Sec) and the social apps (Meetup, Partiful) exist in separate categories. Nobody combines them. Disconnect sits at the intersection.

### Trends Working in Our Favor

1. **"Phone-free" movement** — phone-free concerts (Yondr pouches), phone-free schools, Gen Z "dumbphone" trend
2. **Surgeon General's social isolation advisory** — institutional momentum behind IRL connection
3. **Social media regulation** — age verification laws, potential TikTok ban creating category instability
4. **"Third place" renaissance** — Gen Z rediscovering coffee shops, parks, libraries as social spaces
5. **Anti-algorithm sentiment** — growing backlash against engagement-optimized feeds

## 5. Business Model

### Phase 1: Free (Months 1-6)
Everything is free. Focus on user acquisition, retention, and community density. No monetization.

### Phase 2: Freemium B2C (Months 6-12)

**Disconnect+** — $4.99/month or $39.99/year

| Feature | Free | Disconnect+ |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Browse & join events | Unlimited | Unlimited |
| Create events | 2/month | Unlimited |
| Beacons | 1 active | 3 active |
| Discovery radius | 10 miles | 50 miles |
| Event attendee cap | 20 | 100 |
| Interest categories | 3 max | Unlimited |
| Screen time insights | Weekly total | Daily breakdown + trends |
| Profile badge | — | "Founding Member" badge |
| Priority event visibility | — | Your events rank higher in your area |

**Target conversion rate:** 5-8% of active users (industry avg for social apps is 3-5%)

**Revenue projection (Year 1):**
- 50,000 users × 6% conversion × $40/year = **$120,000 ARR**

### Phase 3: B2B Expansion (Year 2+)

**Disconnect for Communities** — white-label / branded version for:

| Customer | Use Case | Pricing Model |
|----------|---------|---------------|
| Universities | Campus events, orientation, student clubs | $500-2,000/month per campus |
| Coworking spaces | Member meetups, community building | $200-500/month per location |
| City recreation depts | Public events, parks & rec programming | $1,000-5,000/month per city |
| Corporations | Employee social events, team building | $2-5/employee/month |

**Revenue projection (Year 2):**
- B2C: 200K users × 7% × $40 = **$560,000**
- B2B: 50 contracts × $1,000/mo avg = **$600,000**
- **Total Year 2: ~$1.16M ARR**

### Phase 4: Sponsorships (Year 2+)

Local businesses can sponsor event categories or locations:
- "Coffee meetups brought to you by [local roaster]" — $200-500/month per city
- "This park beacon zone sponsored by [city parks dept]" — $100-300/month
- Strict rules: No targeting, no data sharing, no algorithmic placement. Brand presence only.

## 6. Go-to-Market Strategy

### Launch City Selection

**Criteria:**
- High Gen Z density (college town or young-professional city)
- Walkable / transit-accessible (people need to actually get to events)
- Active local culture (coffee shops, parks, community events)
- Not oversaturated with competitor apps

**Top candidates:**
1. **Austin, TX** — huge Gen Z population, tech-savvy, outdoor culture, event culture
2. **Portland, OR** — anti-corporate sentiment aligns with brand, strong community culture
3. **Denver, CO** — young, active, outdoor-oriented, growing tech scene
4. **Madison, WI** — college town, compact, high density of young people

### Pre-Launch (Weeks 1-4)

1. **Landing page + waitlist** — collect email + city, build anticipation
2. **Instagram / TikTok presence** — short-form content about screen time, IRL connection, the paradox of using an app to get off apps
3. **Campus ambassadors** — recruit 10-20 students at 2-3 universities in the launch city
4. **Local partnerships** — coffee shops, parks, community centers as event venues
5. **Press outreach** — pitch to tech blogs (TechCrunch, The Verge) and lifestyle media (local press in launch city)

### Launch (Weeks 5-8)

1. **Seeded events** — team creates 20-30 real events in the first two weeks (coffee meetups, park hangouts, pickup sports)
2. **Ambassador-driven** — campus ambassadors host 5 events each in week 1
3. **Launch party** — one big in-person event to kick off, document for social content
4. **Referral mechanic** — "Invite a friend, both get Disconnect+ for a month free"

### Growth (Months 2-6)

1. **City-by-city expansion** — open waitlist for next city when current city hits 1,000 users
2. **UGC loop** — users share event photos on Instagram/TikTok with #disconnect tag
3. **Word of mouth** — the core growth engine; people bring friends to events, friends download the app
4. **University partnerships** — pitch to student life offices as a community-building tool

### Key Metrics to Track

| Metric | Target (Month 3) | Why It Matters |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Monthly Active Users (MAU) | 1,000 | Critical mass for the network to feel alive |
| Events created / week | 50+ | Supply side — are people creating things to do? |
| RSVP rate | 40%+ | Are people showing up? |
| Show-up rate | 60%+ of RSVPs | The real metric — did they actually go? |
| Avg session length | < 3 min | We WANT this low — proves the product thesis |
| Weekly retention (W1) | 40%+ | Are people coming back? |
| Beacons created / week | 100+ | Spontaneous connection is the differentiator |
| NPS | 50+ | Would they recommend to a friend? |

## 9. Risks & Mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|------------|
| **Low density** — app feels empty without enough users in one area | High | Critical | Seed events aggressively; launch in one dense city; campus ambassador program |
| **Chicken-and-egg** — no events → no users → no events | High | Critical | Team-created events for first month; partner with existing community groups |
| **Safety incident** — minor meets stranger, bad outcome | Medium | Critical | Age restrictions enforced; no DMs; block mechanism; reporting system in v1.1; all-ages tagging |
| **Monetization too early** — users leave when paywall appears | Medium | High | 6 months free; paywall only on creation/power features, not consumption |
| **Copycats** — Meetup, Eventbrite, or a new app copies the anti-screen-time angle | Medium | Medium | Brand moat + community density; first-mover in the specific niche; hard to copy authentic ethos |
| **Regulatory** — COPPA/age verification laws tighten | Low | Medium | Already block under-13; restrict minors; DOB verification at signup |
| **Burnout** — solo founder, building + launching + marketing | High | High | Automate what's possible; hire community manager early; set sustainable pace |

## 11. Why Now?

1. **Cultural moment** — anti-screen-time sentiment is peaking. Schools banning phones, Surgeon General advisory, "dumbphone" trend, phone-free events going mainstream.
2. **Regulatory tailwind** — social media age verification laws and potential TikTok ban create category instability and user openness to alternatives.
3. **Post-pandemic loneliness** — people want IRL connection but have lost the social infrastructure to facilitate it.
4. **No incumbent** — Meetup is stale, IRL shut down, Partiful is invite-only, BeReal is still a screen-time app. The specific niche of "anti-screen-time social discovery" is unoccupied.
5. **Tech is ready** — React Native, PostGIS, Expo, and free-tier cloud services make it possible to build and launch this as a solo developer for under $15K.
